## 3. Officially confirmed challenge structure

Applicants must choose one primary track:

1. **Data Track** — collecting, structuring, documenting, governing and sustaining datasets for AI development in Zimbabwe and the region.
2. **Design Track** — converting structured data into usable dashboards, data stories, visualisations and decision-support experiences; explicitly not a graphic-design contest.
3. **Development Track** — AI-enabled products, prototypes and MVPs.
4. **Deployment Track** — responsible adoption, governance, assurance, monitoring, security, compliance and pilot/deployment models.

### Track positioning decision for ZimLearnGraph

**Official-fit conclusion:** ZimLearnGraph should apply under **Track 1 — Data**. Its primary asset is the proposed collection, curation, governance and reusable handover of curriculum-linked Zimbabwean learning evidence. The live application should be presented as proof that the data is structured and useful, not as the submission's primary innovation.

**Strategic secondary fit:** Design Track principles should inform the demonstration and judge experience, but the application must remain positioned in one primary track.

## 6. Data Track definition and admissibility

### Officially confirmed

Track 1 is a **planning and pilot-collection track**. A complete dataset is not required at application time. Applicants may submit:

- an existing dataset;
- a hybrid submission; or
- a robust data collection proposal.

The proposal must be lawful, ethical and technically credible and explain how data will be collected, structured, validated, protected, documented, governed and handed over as an AI-ready asset.

Strong sourcing evidence may include access confirmations, letters of support, collection approvals, source registers, API details and other proof of feasibility.

### Direct consequence for ZimLearnGraph

The strongest missing evidence is not another dashboard feature. It is **credible collection access**: named pilot schools or equivalent access evidence, permission status, collection roles, sample size and a realistic collection schedule.

## 7. Official Data Track scoring

| Criterion | Weight | What official reviewers assess |
|---|---:|---|
| **C1 — Collection Feasibility and Sourcing Logic** | **40%** | Access to sources/collection environment, clear methods, sample relevance, operational feasibility and source limitations |
| **C2 — Data Structure, Quality and Machine-Readiness** | **30%** | Schema, dictionary, metadata, validation, quality control, labelling, interoperability and handover |
| **C3 — Ethics, Compliance, Governance and Responsible Use** | **30%** | Lawful basis, consent, privacy, anonymisation, security, bias/representativeness, licensing/access and stewardship |

### Exemplary band

- C1: **36–40** — verified or highly credible access, clear collection and sampling logic, realistic operations and understood source limits.
- C2: **27–30** — clear structure, dictionary, metadata, validation, quality, labels where relevant, and machine-readable handover.
- C3: **27–30** — strong Data Protection Act compliance, consent/lawful basis, anonymisation, security, representativeness, controlled access/licensing and stewardship.

## 8. Required Track 1 written proposal

### Format

- Formal proposal uploaded in addition to the portal questionnaire.
- Maximum **10 pages**, excluding cover and technical appendices.
- **PDF only**; editable Word or other formats may be disqualified.
- **Avenir or Arial, 11pt, 1.15 line spacing, 1-inch margins**.
- Filename: `[ProjectID]_AI4I_Proposal_Data.pdf`.

### Required structure

1. **Cover page** — project title, track, team, lead innovator, date; not counted.
2. **Problem Definition and Strategic Alignment** — local problem, intended AI use, users/beneficiaries and national alignment.
3. **Data Source, Collection and Access Plan** — sources, collection, permissions, sampling, expected size and limitations.
4. **Data Structure, Quality and AI-Readiness Plan** — schema, dictionary, metadata, formats, labels, validation, representativeness and handover.
5. **Compliance, Privacy, Security and Risk Management** — lawful/ethical basis, consent, de-identification, Data Protection Act compliance, storage, access and mitigations.
6. **Sustainability, Stewardship and Future Adoption** — steward, maintenance/refresh, licensing or controlled access, costs/budget and adoption.

## 9. Required technical content and evidence

### Explicit Track 1 requirements

The proposal must address:

- intended AI/analytical use, users, local relevance and public value;
- source, provenance, collection, permissions and limitations;
- schema, data dictionary, metadata and file organisation;
- quality controls, validation, correction and acceptance criteria;
- labelling/annotation where relevant;
- bias and representativeness across relevant Zimbabwean contexts;
- privacy, consent, anonymisation, security and Data Protection Act compliance;
- machine-readable handover for AI workflows, analytics, APIs or controlled compute;
- sustainability, stewardship, licensing, updates and adoption.

### Official minimum handover package

The guidance expects a credible plan/sample for:

- `README.md` or PDF;
- structured metadata (`metadata.json`/YAML) per subset;
- `data_dictionary.csv`/XLSX;
- machine-readable schema (`schema.json`, SQL DDL or equivalent);
- separated `raw/`, `processed/`, `labels/`, `validation/`, `governance/` folders;
- `manifest.csv` with every file's path, size, checksum, version and sensitivity;
- validation report, rules and issue/error log;
- consent, anonymisation, access and licence documents;
- load test demonstrating sample data opens in Python, R, SQL or an appropriate standard tool **without manual editing**.

## 10. Official AI-ready minimum expectations

The official guidance defines AI-ready data as lawful, documented, structured, validated, representative and machine-readable—not merely collected.

Required quality dimensions:

- completeness;
- accuracy;
- consistency;
- validity;
- uniqueness;
- timeliness;
- integrity.

Notable official guidance:

- Critical fields should ordinarily reach **95% completeness unless justified**.
- Dates should use ISO 8601.
- Controlled vocabularies are preferred over uncontrolled free text.
- Cleaning transformations should be logged.
- Validation should combine automated checks and human review.
- Validation needs pass/warning/fail conditions and correction ownership.
- Personal and child-related data require strong oversight, privacy controls, access logs, bias review, security and expert validation.
- Open release is not automatically appropriate; restricted, anonymised, aggregated, delayed or controlled access may be required.

## 11. Responsible-AI expectations

### Officially confirmed or guided

- Comply with Zimbabwe's Data Protection Act [Chapter 12:07].
- State lawful basis and consent workflow where required.
- Minimise personal information.
- De-identify/anonymise appropriately.
- Define access, retention, deletion, storage, transfer and incident handling.
- Assess geographic, language, institutional, device and other relevant representativeness risks.
- Explain intended and non-intended use.
- Use human oversight for high-impact education decisions.
- Do not present uncertain AI output as final truth.
- Keep auditable records where impact warrants it.

### ZimLearnGraph interpretation

Deterministic analytics must remain the source of reported metrics. Generative AI should only summarise or suggest teacher-reviewable actions and must not autonomously punish, exclude, promote, discipline or rank children, teachers or schools.

## 17. Judge expectations vs strategic recommendations

### Reasonable judge expectations grounded in official guidance

- A judge can trace a dashboard metric back to a source mark and transformation.
- A safe sample loads without manual repair.
- Data access and rights are credible, not hypothetical.
- Seeded demonstration content is visibly disclosed.
- Known limitations are candid.
- Team can explain why deterministic rules are used where sufficient and AI remains advisory.
- Low-connectivity collection is operationally plausible.

### Strategic recommendations (not official rules)

1. Spend the remaining pre-deadline effort first on a **submission-complete 10-page PDF**, exact portal-field capture, and one credible school-access letter or documented collection pathway.
2. Produce a small, safe **dataset release v0.1-demo** with dictionary CSV, metadata JSON, schema, manifest/checksums, validation report and loading test.
3. Correct current documentation that describes planned quality/release/ingestion capabilities as already operational.
4. Fix any runtime report/export defects that could fail during judging.
5. Use the live UI to make lineage, quality and readiness visible; do not attempt the full national platform before 14 July.
6. Treat the major visual overhaul as a phased product programme after challenge-critical evidence is secured, while delivering a focused public/demo polish slice before submission if time permits.
